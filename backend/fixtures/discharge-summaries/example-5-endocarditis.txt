Example 5 — Infective Endocarditis With
Heart Failure and Prolonged IV Antibiotic
Therapy
Patient: 58-year-old woman
Admission diagnosis: Native mitral-valve infective endocarditis complicated by
acute heart failure
Discharge diagnoses:
• Mitral-valve infective endocarditis
• Acute on chronic heart failure with reduced EF
• Pulmonary edema, resolved
• Chronic kidney disease
• Anemia of chronic disease
• History of intravenous drug use, in remission
Hospital Course
The patient was admitted with fever, malaise, progressive dyspnea, and lower-
extremity edema. Blood cultures repeatedly grew methicillin-
susceptible Staphylococcus aureus. Echocardiography demonstrated a mitral-valve
vegetation with significant mitral regurgitation.
She was initially treated with empiric IV antibiotics and subsequently narrowed to a
pathogen-directed IV antibiotic regimen after infectious-disease consultation.
Cardiothoracic surgery evaluated the patient and recommended continued medical
therapy with close outpatient follow-up at this time.
Her hospitalization was complicated by acute pulmonary edema and decompensated
systolic heart failure. She required aggressive IV diuresis with subsequent clinical
improvement. Guideline-directed heart-failure therapy was cautiously restarted as
blood pressure and renal function permitted.
At discharge, she was afebrile with cleared follow-up blood cultures, improved
respiratory status, and stable renal function. She requires completion of a prolonged
course of IV antimicrobial therapy through a supervised outpatient program.
Discharge Medications
New medications
• Cefazolin IV at the infectious-disease-directed dose and schedule, to
complete the planned endocarditis treatment course.
• Furosemide 40 mg by mouth once daily for ongoing volume management.
• Losartan 25 mg once daily, initiated at a low dose because of improving blood
pressure and renal function.
Continue
• Metoprolol succinate at the discharge dose.
• Other chronic medications after final medication reconciliation.
Stopped
• Empiric inpatient antibiotics that were no longer indicated after culture-directed
therapy.
• NSAIDs — avoid unless specifically approved.
Discharge Instructions
• Complete the entire IV antibiotic course.
• Attend all infectious-disease and cardiology follow-up appointments.
• Obtain laboratory monitoring according to the outpatient antimicrobial-
treatment plan.
• Keep vascular-access/PICC care appointments and follow instructions for the
line.
• Do not independently stop or change IV antibiotics.
• Monitor daily weight and symptoms of heart failure.
• Follow a low-sodium diet and prescribed fluid plan.
• Follow any activity restrictions or dental/invasive-procedure recommendations
provided by the infectious-disease/cardiology teams.
Return Precautions
Seek urgent medical attention for:
• Fever, chills, or recurrent systemic illness.
• New or worsening shortness of breath.
• Chest pain.
• Fainting or severe dizziness.
• New neurologic symptoms such as weakness, facial droop, difficulty speaking,
or confusion.
• Rapidly worsening edema or weight gain.
• Bleeding or significant drainage/redness around the vascular-access site.
• Sudden severe back, chest, or abdominal pain.
• Any concern for a complication related to the IV line.
Nursing Discharge Teaching / What Nursing Typically Relays
Nursing typically reinforces:
• The exact antibiotic schedule and duration, while emphasizing that the full
course must be completed.
• PICC/vascular-access care and infection-prevention practices.
• Which symptoms could indicate recurrent infection or heart failure.
• Daily weights and when to contact the clinical team.
• Medication reconciliation, particularly new heart-failure medications and
discontinued inpatient antibiotics.
• Laboratory and infusion appointments.
• Who to contact after discharge for medication/line problems.
• Emergency symptoms requiring immediate evaluation.
• Teach-back: patient demonstrates understanding of antibiotic
administration/line-care instructions or confirms the responsible caregiver can
perform them correctly.
• Confirmation that home services, infusion supplies, transportation, and follow-
up appointments are arranged before discharge.
