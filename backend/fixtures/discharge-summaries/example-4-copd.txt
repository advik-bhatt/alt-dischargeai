Example 4 — COPD Exacerbation With
Pneumonia and Acute on Chronic Heart
Failure
Patient: 69-year-old man
Admission diagnosis: Acute hypoxic respiratory failure due to COPD exacerbation,
pneumonia, and volume overload
Discharge diagnoses:
• COPD exacerbation
• Community-acquired pneumonia
• Acute on chronic systolic heart failure
• Acute hypoxic respiratory failure, resolved
• Hypertension
• Tobacco use disorder
Hospital Course
The patient presented with increasing cough, wheezing, purulent sputum, and
progressive dyspnea. He required supplemental oxygen on admission. Chest imaging
demonstrated a left lower lobe infiltrate and pulmonary vascular congestion.
He was treated with systemic corticosteroids, bronchodilator therapy, antibiotics, and
IV diuresis. Respiratory status gradually improved, and he was transitioned back to
his baseline respiratory regimen. He was able to maintain appropriate oxygenation on
his prescribed home oxygen requirement prior to discharge.
Heart-failure medications were adjusted because of borderline blood pressure during
hospitalization. Diuretic dosing was increased temporarily, with improvement in
volume status.
Discharge Medications
New medications
• Prednisone 40 mg by mouth daily for 2 additional days to complete COPD
exacerbation treatment.
• Doxycycline 100 mg by mouth twice daily for 3 additional days to complete
antibiotic treatment.
• Furosemide 40 mg by mouth once daily, increased from prior dose for
volume management.
Continue
• Maintenance inhaler as reconciled.
• Rescue albuterol inhaler as needed.
• Carvedilol at the discharge dose.
• Other chronic medications as listed on the final medication reconciliation.
Stopped
• Previous lower-dose furosemide regimen, replaced by the new dose.
• Any duplicate inhaler therapy identified during medication reconciliation.
Discharge Instructions
• Complete prednisone and doxycycline courses.
• Use maintenance inhaler every day as prescribed; rescue inhaler is for
breakthrough symptoms.
• Use home oxygen at the prescribed flow rate and during the prescribed
activities/sleep periods.
• Continue heart-failure monitoring, including daily weights.
• Follow a low-sodium diet.
• Strongly recommend smoking cessation.
• Follow up with primary care, pulmonology, and cardiology as arranged.
Return Precautions
Seek emergency care for:
• Severe shortness of breath not relieved by prescribed rescue treatment.
• New or worsening chest pain.
• Blue/gray lips or severe difficulty speaking because of breathlessness.
• New confusion or inability to stay awake.
• Significant increase in oxygen requirement.
• High fever or rapidly worsening productive cough.
• Rapid weight gain with worsening edema or orthopnea.
Nursing Discharge Teaching / What Nursing Typically Relays
Nursing would generally reinforce:
• Correct use of maintenance versus rescue inhalers.
• Completion of antibiotics and steroids.
• Oxygen safety and prescribed use.
• Smoking-cessation counseling/resources.
• Daily weights and fluid/low-sodium instructions.
• Recognition of worsening respiratory symptoms.
• Correct furosemide dose and expected urinary frequency.
• Follow-up appointments.
• Confirmation that the patient has oxygen, inhalers, medications, and
appropriate transportation/support at home.
